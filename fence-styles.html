Expert Guidance

How to Choose the Right Fence Style

Three things to consider before you pick a style — learned from 25 years on the job.

Match Your Architecture

A horizontal slat fence suits a mid-century modern home. A white vinyl picket fits a cottage. A stately aluminum fence complements a colonial estate. Style should feel intentional, not accidental.

Decide Your Privacy Level

Full board and vinyl give 100% privacy. Horizontal slat is around 90%. Aluminum and chain link are see-through. Be honest about how much privacy you actually need — it drives both style and cost.

Budget Beyond Year One

Cedar is warm but needs staining. Vinyl costs more upfront but zero maintenance. Aluminum lasts a lifetime but has a higher install cost. Think about total cost of ownership, not just the quote.

Style & Code Guide

Fence Styles, HOA Codes & Permitting FAQ

Essential guidance on orientation, height limits, HOA submissions, and zoning bylaws to help you choose the right fence with confidence.

Which side of the fence has to face my neighbor?

Under municipal "Good Neighbor" ordinances and standard building conventions, the finished, smooth side of pickets must face outward toward adjacent properties and the street, with structural rails and posts remaining on the inside. Double-sided board-on-board and horizontal styles offer finished boards on both sides so both you and your neighbors enjoy an identical, flawless view.

Apex Pro Tip: Board-on-Board avoids neighbor disputes entirely.

What are the standard residential height restrictions?

Standard zoning codes permit up to 6 feet in rear and side yards for private outdoor living spaces, and up to 3 or 4 feet in front yards to preserve vehicular line-of-sight near driveways and intersections. Corner lots often require specialized sight-triangle setbacks. We verify every township code before finalizing your layout.

Apex Pro Tip: 8ft privacy fences are permitted with variance approval.

What styles meet swimming pool enclosure safety codes?

National BOCA and International Building Code (IBC) pool regulations mandate a minimum fence height of 48 to 54 inches, picket spacing narrower than 4 inches, and no exterior footholds. Gates must swing outward away from the water and feature self-closing hinges with magnetic latches mounted at least 54 inches high. Our Ornamental Aluminum and Solid Vinyl lines are 100% pool-code certified.

Apex Pro Tip: 54" Flat-Top Aluminum is the #1 choice for pools.

Can I combine two different fence styles on one property?

Blending styles is one of the most effective ways to balance curb appeal and budget. Many homeowners choose decorative black ornamental aluminum along front boundaries and pool areas to preserve scenic views, while transitioning seamlessly to 6ft cedar or vinyl privacy fencing along property lines where seclusion is needed.

Apex Pro Tip: Custom transition posts bridge wood to aluminum cleanly.

How do I handle HOA Architectural Review Board approval?

Most planned developments require an application package with material cut sheets, height specifications, property survey maps, and finish colors. Apex Fence Solutions provides pre-packaged HOA submission packets with stamped engineering specifications, helping 99% of our clients receive first-round approval without delays.

Apex Pro Tip: We draft and sign HOA review packets at no added charge.

How are posts anchored to prevent wind lean and frost heave?

Every post is machine-augured 36 to 42 inches into the ground—well below local winter frost lines. We pour 120 lbs of high-density commercial concrete per post with bell-shaped bottom footings that anchor firmly against ground shifting, ensuring straight, plumb posts that withstand sustained 100+ mph gusts.

Apex Pro Tip: Backed by our Lifetime Gate Sag & Post Stability Guarantee.
